What fire alarm systems do we install?

We install conventional fire alarm systems for smaller properties, grouping detectors into zones for easy identification.

For larger properties, we recommend addressable fire alarm systems where each detector has a unique address, allowing exact pinpointing of the triggered device to improve response times.

What a fire alarm system can include

  • Smoke detectors (photoelectric / ionisation)
  • Heat detectors
  • Manual call points
  • Fire alarm control panel
  • Audible alarm sounders
  • Visual alarm indicators (strobes)
  • Zone planning and layout
  • Battery backup power
  • System testing and commissioning
  • Maintenance support

Design considerations

What to consider when planning a fire alarm system

Property type

Building use and occupancy

Different areas require different detectors. Kitchens use heat detectors to prevent false alarms from steam, while bedrooms require early-warning smoke detectors.

Zone planning

Detection zone layout

In a conventional system, zones group detectors by logical areas, like floors or sections, helping responders locate the source quickly.

Power

Mains power and battery backup

Fire alarms require continuous power with sufficient battery backup to keep the system operational during a mains failure.

Maintenance

Ongoing testing and maintenance

Systems need periodic testing of detectors, sounders, call points, and batteries to ensure everything functions perfectly in an emergency.

Common questions

Questions about fire alarm installation

What detector is suitable for a kitchen?

Heat detectors are best for kitchens. They activate based on rapid temperature rises, preventing false alarms from cooking steam or smoke.

Can it link to access control?

Yes, we can integrate the systems so access-controlled doors release automatically when a fire alarm is triggered, ensuring a safe exit.

Does it need regular servicing?

Yes. Periodic testing of detectors, batteries, and call points is crucial for confirming the system will respond correctly in a fire event.

Smoke vs Heat detectors?

Smoke detectors provide early warnings and are great for living spaces. Heat detectors trigger on temperature changes and are best for kitchens or dusty environments.
